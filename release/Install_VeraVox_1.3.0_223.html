VeraVox 1.3.0

A wide update: playback settings, the queue, the Library drawer, and a long list of smaller things. Your library, queues, saved playlists and settings are untouched, and there is nothing you need to do.

Playback

  • Bit Depth in Settings is now a choice of three: Match source, Prefer 24-bit (the default, and what VeraVox has always done) and Maximum. With Match source a 16-bit file reaches the DAC as 16 bits, and a DAC that displays its input depth shows the file's. If you had Max Bit Depth switched on you will find Maximum selected; if you change nothing, nothing changes.
  • An amber line under the picker may say that two of the settings deliver the same depth on your DAC, at some sample rates or at all, even when the DAC's format list shows that depth. It is not a fault. What counts is not only which depths a DAC lists but how each one is laid out: VeraVox keeps every sample in a 32-bit word, Prefer 24-bit and Maximum take only the formats that word is copied into as it is, and a 24-bit format packed into three bytes is not one of them, so on such a DAC both deliver 32 bits. Match source is the one setting that repacks to reach the file's exact depth, and where the DAC has no 16-bit format it lands where Prefer 24-bit does. In every case your bits travel unmodified, with zeros below them when the word is wider: bit-perfect either way. The line only tells you in advance which settings your DAC cannot tell apart.
  • A 24-bit ALAC on a DAC that offers only 16-bit exclusive formats at that rate used to go out truncated under the Bit-Perfect label. It now takes the Shared path and says so. Nothing changes for any other file.
  • Audio interfaces with no 2-channel output format, as most recording interfaces are, could not play FLAC, WavPack and RF64 files: noise in Exclusive, several times too fast in Shared. They play normally on both paths now; in Exclusive the Signal Path shows Enhanced with a Format Conversion step.
  • The DAC Diagnostic panel's table of physical formats gains a Packing column, and the tag beside the format says when the Bit Depth choice made no difference at that rate.

The queue

  • Each row reads like a Library row: artist and album under the title, a short format badge such as 24/96 or DSD64, the duration in a straight column, and the title with all the room at every width. Composer and year follow the same Settings switch as the Library.
  • The heart and the stars are live, as in Now Playing. A title that does not fit unfolds whole when you rest the pointer on it.
  • Right-click a row: Play, Play Next, Move to Top, Move to End, Remove from Queue, Favorites and Rating, Add to Saved Playlist, Show in Library, Get Info, Show in Finder. With several rows selected it acts on all of them and says how many.
  • The Web Remote's queue shows the album and the same short badge.

The Library and its drawer

  • The Library comes back on the tab you were using: after a relaunch, after ⌘L, and after moving it between the drawer and its own window.
  • The drawer can be as wide as the window allows. The album grid shows as many columns as fit at your cover size, and the zoom slider appears in the drawer's status bar once there is room for it. At the usual widths nothing changes.
  • The drawer can no longer be squeezed until its title and search field are cut off. If you had saved a narrower drawer, it opens at the new minimum.
  • The summary line under the Library reads whole at any width, and while you search it counts what the tab is showing rather than matches hidden in genre, composer, year or comments.

Booklets and the Web Remote

  • If an album folder holds more than one PDF, you see them all: tabs above the pages on the Mac (⌃Tab and ⌃⇧Tab step through them), chips on the Web Remote. A box set with a booklet per disc lists all of them from the album page.
  • On the Web Remote, a cover you have changed updates without restarting VeraVox.

Network renderers and the diagnostic report

  • A renderer that states its service addresses in relative form, as the UPnP standard prescribes, used to appear in the picker and answer no command. It works now; renderers that already worked are untouched.
  • The diagnostic report stays readable after hours on the network: a silent device is reported once rather than every five minutes, a device announcing itself under a dozen names appears as one, a control point polling for position is one line per streak, and a Mac that cannot send multicast says so once. Nothing changes on the network or in the audio.

Backups

  • Restoring a backup made by an older VeraVox brings the library up to today's model, where before compilations could come back split, or some covers and tags missing. If you never restore a backup, nothing changes.
  • A search index that has fallen behind the library is now noticed at launch and rebuilt in the background.

Several of the improvements above began with someone taking the time to write in and describe what they saw.
