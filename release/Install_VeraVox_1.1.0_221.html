VeraVox 1.1.0

A larger update than the last two. Your library, queues, saved playlists and settings are untouched, and there is nothing to rebuild. Two things are worth knowing before you start.

The way albums are grouped has been corrected, and VeraVox applies the correction to your existing library by itself the first time you open it after updating. It takes a couple of seconds and asks nothing of you.

VeraVox also reads a few things from your files that it did not read before: the name of a disc inside a box set, the codec actually inside an .m4a, the tags of a file described by a CUE sheet. Those appear on albums the scanner reads again, so on an existing library you will see them after a Deep Re-scan, or after a Rescan of a single folder from the Folder Manager.

Speed

  • Adding a folder to the library is substantially faster, on a local disk and over a network drive alike, and so is every update after it. VeraVox reads far less of each file to learn what it needs, so the saving is largest where the reading was slowest. How long your own library takes still depends on where the music lives and on what sits between VeraVox and it. You can also start listening sooner: the first tracks become playable while the rest of the folder is still being read. Track lengths read from FLAC files are exact now, where they could be overstated by a fraction of a second.
  • Adding a track to a long queue, reordering it, or loading a saved playlist onto it is immediate, whatever the length of the queue and wherever the music lives. On a library held on a network drive those gestures could hold VeraVox still for a long while, and gestures made in quick succession piled the waits up.
  • When the VeraVox window is covered, minimised, or the app is hidden, its animations stop and resume untouched when you bring it back. Listening with VeraVox in the background now costs a small fraction of what it did. The analysis instruments are unaffected and go on measuring either way.

Albums and box sets

  • An album whose tracks name different artists, and which carries no album artist of its own, used to appear split into one album per artist: a soundtrack, or a classical record with two orchestras. It now stays whole, under Various Artists, without anything being written to your files. If you tag your own album artists, nothing changes for you, by design. A new switch in Settings, Group Untagged Compilations, is on by default and turning it off restores the previous grouping exactly and immediately.
  • A box set ripped as one file per disc with a CUE sheet beside each one appeared as one album per disc, titled after the disc, with no disc number, genre or year. It is now a single album with its real title, its Disc 1 and Disc 2 sections, and the metadata the files declare. Single disc rips made the same way gain their genre and year too.
  • A box set whose discs have names of their own shows the name beside the number, so Disc 1 reads as the disc is called, including when that name is declared in the form older ID3 tags are obliged to use.
  • Moving a box set, or any folder with subfolders inside it, to the Trash makes it leave the Library on its own within seconds, as flat album folders already did. It used to stay behind as a ghost album until the next update.
  • Sorting by Year, or by Album Artist and Year, puts albums that share a year in the order they were released rather than in alphabetical order. This applies where your files declare a full date; with the year alone nothing changes.
  • Updating the library is faster, and an album copied in while VeraVox is watching no longer arrives without its title or cover. Every update used to re-read hundreds of files that had not changed. A file already in your library that arrived without its tags, which neither an update nor a Deep Re-scan could repair before, is recovered by a Deep Re-scan now.

What VeraVox tells you about a file

  • The Signal Path names the codec that is really inside the file. A track bought from a store, an AAC inside an .m4a, used to read ALAC: a claim of lossless decoding over a compressed file, on the one screen that exists to tell the truth about the signal. It now reads AAC and says the decode is lossy, and the Library rows, album cards, Track Info and Web Remote say the same. The Lossless, Lossy and Hi-Res filters classify by the real codec, so an AAC album in an .m4a leaves Lossless and appears under Lossy, where you would look for it.
  • The panel listing supported formats names M4A, the extension ALAC and AAC arrive with from an iTunes or Apple Music library. Those files always opened and played; the list simply did not mention them.
  • A dropout caused by a starved buffer is recorded on every playback path and appears in the exported diagnostic log, even if the Stream Inspector was closed at the time. If you report one, the evidence is inside the file you send, without anyone having to ask you to keep a panel open while you listen.

Fixes

  • In shuffle towards certain network players, HQPlayer 6 among them, playback stopped by itself at the end of a track. Play restarted the same track and stopped again at the same point, and only Next moved things on. The queue now advances by itself with those players too.
  • With the Tuneshine® panel enabled and the display unreachable, every track change held the interface still for several seconds, and the Sample Rate Switch Pause countdown appeared already half spent. Neither happens now, and covers start appearing again on their own as soon as the display can be reached.
  • On macOS 27 beta the Settings popover appeared visibly broken, with titles cut off under the left edge and two of its cards overflowing on the right. It is whole again, on macOS 26 and on macOS 27 beta alike.
  • The note and tip boxes in Help follow the width of the column, at any window width and any text size, instead of standing as wide as their own text.

Most of what is above came from someone taking the time to describe what they saw.
