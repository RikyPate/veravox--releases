VeraVox 1.6.0

Settings in a window of their own, a search for the queue, smooth fades and gapless playback for every format, and a deep overhaul of how VeraVox works with network renderers. Your library, queues, saved playlists and settings are untouched, except for the Dynamic Range figures of DSF files: see below.

Settings and the player

  • Every setting now lives in one Settings window, built like the Help: open it with ⌘, or the gear, keep it beside the player, and search it by word. Each card has a ? that opens its page in the Help. The output and its Playback Mode, Group by Work and the time display are there too, and the Web Remote panel is now a card, still at ⇧⌘R.
  • The player names the output it plays on, next to the output button, and the Web Remote shows it too. The icon beside it always tells the truth: an open padlock when VeraVox will take the DAC at Play, a closed one while it holds it, a speaker when the DAC is shared. The mode is now called Exclusive, and you choose it in the device picker or in Settings: Tools ▸ Playback Mode is gone.
  • The space bar plays and pauses, in the main window and in every panel. The info and booklet icons over the cover appear when you point at it.

The queue

  • Find in Queue, with ⌘F or the magnifier next to Queue, jumps to the first matching row; Return or ⌘G goes to the next, and Esc leaves that row selected, ready to play. The Web Remote's queue has the same search.

Playback

  • Play, pause, seek, Stop and every change of track that is not gapless now fade in or out over 50 milliseconds, on every format and output, and DSD over DoP fades as cleanly as PCM. The reason is protection: a stream that starts or stops abruptly sends a sudden jump through your DAC to the amplifier and the speakers, and now your system only ever receives music, silence or a smooth ramp between the two. A slow source, such as a busy NAS, fades out, waits and picks up exactly where it stopped.
  • Gapless playback works the same for every format and output path, DSD included, and SACD ISO tracks begin and end exactly where the disc says. DSF and DFF files start without being read in full, which helps from a NAS.
  • In Exclusive, VeraVox still sets your DAC to each file's own rate whenever the DAC offers it, with nothing converted. What changes is Shared Output chosen on a DAC that also offers Exclusive: VeraVox now leaves the DAC at the rate it has and converts the file itself, at its highest quality, with the step shown in the Signal Path. If another app changes that rate during playback, the music carries on.
  • The Signal Path no longer calls the signal lossless where VeraVox can't guarantee it, as with a converted file or a wireless output.
  • A virtual audio device that accepts exclusive formats, such as the bridge some audio software installs, is now Exclusive Ready like a DAC: VeraVox asks at the first Play whether to use it in Exclusive or Shared Output, and you can change the answer in the device picker or in Settings ▸ Output.
  • DSD1024 files of both rate families now play, and each DSD rate has one name throughout the app.

Dynamic Range and the Booklet

  • A DSF file usually ends with a short block of filler that isn't music, and the Dynamic Range analysis used to read it as part of the track, which could skew the figures, above all on quiet tracks. It now measures the music alone. So that every figure comes from the same measurement, the DR values of DSF tracks from earlier versions are cleared once, at the first launch: right-click an album and choose Compute Dynamic Range to measure it again, or measure everything at once with Tools ▸ Analyze Library Dynamic Range…, which on a large collection may take a while.
  • The Booklet finds documents kept in a box set's folder, whatever the disc folders are called. Their icons light up after the next library update.

Network renderers

  • VeraVox now follows the UPnP standard closely, as a control point and in UPnP Renderer Mode. Renderers appear sooner and stay listed, your last renderer is the output again right after launch, and the progress bar follows what the renderer actually plays. The player says plainly when a track cannot start, and why, and follows what the renderer does on its own, such as a Stop from its remote or another app taking it over. A CUE album plays on any renderer that accepts WAV, whatever the format of its file.
  • In Renderer Mode, VeraVox shows up in control apps with its icon and reappears as soon as the Mac wakes or the mode is switched back on. A track it can't play is refused at once, so the control app can say why, and a seek beyond what has arrived waits for it. It no longer offers a volume control it never applied: set the volume on your DAC or amplifier.
  • VeraVox holds to the UPnP specifications, and will keep holding to them ever more closely: that rigour is what keeps it dependable among the many ways devices can drift from the standard. As a result, a renderer that departs from it may no longer appear in the list. If one of yours disappears or behaves differently, please send us the log from Help ▸ Export Diagnostic Log…. If the cause lies in VeraVox, we will fix it promptly, within the specifications; if it lies in the renderer or in the control app, we will make a point of reporting it to its maker.

Web Remote

  • The colours of the app's window, a Library header like the Mac's, a waveform that always reaches the end of the bar with a thin line where the played part ends, and screens that start below the status bar on iPad and iPhone.

Other fixes

  • More reliable stops, skips and seeks with DSD files, and when a DAC is unplugged during playback.
  • De-emphasis for CD rips that declare it is applied the same way on every output path.
  • Albums spread over two disc images or two CUE sheets play one disc after the other, a folder dropped on the queue arrives album by album, and every track of a SACD ISO can join the queue.
  • A Speaker Layout stays in place after a seek. FLAC files that macOS can't open show their real sample rate, bit depth and channels, and a file that can't be added to the library is named in the Diagnostic Log, with the reason.
  • VeraVox is more robust against malformed messages from other devices on the network, as a renderer and as a control point.

Many of these changes began with a message from someone who uses VeraVox: thank you for writing in.
