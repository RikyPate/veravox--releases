VeraVox 1.2.0

A deep update rather than a wide one. Your library, queues, saved playlists and settings are untouched, and there is nothing you need to do.

The first time you scroll through the library after updating, VeraVox quietly rebuilds its cover thumbnails in a sharper format as it goes. It happens on that first pass and never again.

A few of the corrections below concern what VeraVox reads from your files. Those settle on albums the scanner reads again, so on an existing library you will see them after a Deep Re-scan, or a Rescan of a single folder from the Folder Manager.

Playback

  • VeraVox now starts a track with a real cushion of audio ready, and keeps that cushion topped up while the track plays. On a slow disk, a busy network share, or a drive that stops answering for a moment, the start of a track and any hesitation during it could come out as silence while the progress bar kept moving. On a fast local disk you will notice no difference, because there was nothing to wait for.
  • Dragging the progress bar in the last seconds of a track inside a gapless album now lands exactly where you put it.
  • Playing to a network renderer that moves to the next track by itself no longer stops at the track change. VeraVox used to read that moment as the renderer having nothing to play, when it was already loading the right track.

The library, while you scroll it

  • Scrolling through covers no longer shows you each one arriving, and going back over ground you had already covered no longer reloads them. At the largest cover size the artwork is sharper.
  • Fast scrolling in the detached Library window keeps up with your hand. Covers now sit on a quiet ground instead of inside a glass card, and selecting an album tints the ground around the cover rather than the cover itself.
  • Every artist has one tile again. An artist could appear in two places in the alphabet at once, with their albums and track count split between the two.
  • The A-Z index lands where it says it will. Sorted by Album Artist it often jumped somewhere else, and artists whose name begins with an accented letter had no letter to reach them by. The same applies in All Tracks and in the Web Remote.

What the library remembers

  • An album made of one file per disc plus a CUE sheet no longer jumps back to the top of Recently Added every time you re-scan, and albums from SACD ISO images stop after one Rescan from the Folder Manager. A Dynamic Range figure you had already measured is no longer thrown away with it. Dates already lost cannot be recovered, but from here on they stay put.
  • The Booklet button no longer disappears from tracks whose tags you have edited in place.

Finding your way around

  • The library now says in words what is filtering and sorting it, in a strip above the albums: each active filter as a pill you can lift with a click, and the sort order whenever you have moved it off its default. Before, the only sign was a dot on the filter icon, which is not a thing you look at while scrolling. The command to sort Tags, until now reachable only from the Web Remote, is on the Mac too.
  • The A-Z index has been redesigned: quiet letters at rest, a translucent capsule under the pointer, and a glass lens on the letter you are dragging to, with a callout showing it large.
  • Right-clicking a folder in Music Folders opens the VeraVox menu, as it does everywhere else, and so does the row's own button. On saved playlists the menu appears at the button you pressed.
  • Changing between light and dark with the Library window detached no longer clears your filters, search, tab and open album. The other panels follow a theme change straight away.
  • The resizable tool windows go full screen.

The queue

  • The queue says how many tracks it holds and how long it runs, and while it plays, how much of it is left. Select some rows and it tells you how long the selection lasts.
  • A track dragged to the bottom now lands last, including when you drop it in the empty space below the list, and a line shows where it will land.
  • A long album reads as 1:44:23 rather than 104:23, and so does a track that runs over an hour.

The Web Remote

  • The transport bar shows the same waveform you see on the Mac, filling from the left while the track is analysed. Drag to seek and the coloured part follows your finger. The ABS/ADP pill beside the duration is the Mac's, and the same preference: change it in one place and it changes in the other.
  • Tap the number to the right of the bar to switch between total duration and time remaining. Same preference as the Mac.
  • The A-Z index matches the desktop's, and the letter you are on lights up with a callout beside it, so your finger does not hide where you are going.

Smaller things

  • The Help section on DSD to PCM now explains how memory behaves on that path, so you can tell in advance what a long file will ask of your Mac.
  • The icon beside the output selector reads properly in light theme, and the two network states, VeraVox sending to a renderer and VeraVox receiving as one, share the colour everything on the network uses. The icon tells them apart: an aerial when audio arrives here, a globe when it leaves.
  • The tool icons across the top are grey exactly when they cannot be pressed.
  • Help → Release Notes opens the full history of what changed in every version, newest first, so notes you updated past are no longer out of reach. The same page is public on veravox.audio, and Sparkle offers a Version History button when you check for updates and are already up to date.

Several of the improvements above began with someone taking the time to describe what they saw.
