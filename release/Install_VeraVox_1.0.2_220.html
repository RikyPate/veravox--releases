VeraVox 1.0.2

An ordinary update. Your library, queues, saved playlists and settings are untouched, and there is nothing to rebuild or re-scan afterwards.

Playback

  • Tracks played through Shared Output could stop before their end, and the next one would start. It happened when the output device was sitting at a sample rate higher than the file's, and it affected MP3, AAC, ALAC, WAV and AIFF: a 44.1 kHz track ended at about 46% of itself on a device set to 96 kHz. The time display kept showing the full duration, so a track simply ended before a number that was right, and seeking inside one of them landed somewhere other than where it was asked to. Both are fixed. FLAC, WavPack, DSD and RF64 files were never affected, and neither was Exclusive Ready playback.

Appearance

  • The Appearance menu marks the theme in use, so you can see which of the three is active and that your choice was applied. The sun and moon button now reads what is actually on screen: under the system theme on a dark Mac it offers the light one, where before it offered dark and pressing it changed nothing. The Analog and Modern choice for the instruments works the same way.

Audio Analysis

  • When a file is called out as fake hi-res for two reasons at once, the sentence explaining that this is a measurement and not an accusation can be read in full. Its last line used to be cut in half.
  • The numbers at the top and bottom of the spectrum's dB scale can be read. They were cut across the middle. No grid line has moved, so the graph says exactly what it said before.

Smaller things

  • Opening a smart playlist and coming back leaves you where you were in the list, rather than at the top. Albums, Artists, Genres, Composers and Tags already worked that way; Smart Playlists was the one section that did not.
  • Adding a tag in the Web Remote offers the tags already in your library, the way the desktop editor does. Type a fragment, or tap a name, instead of remembering the exact spelling on a phone or tablet.

Most of what is above came from someone taking the time to describe what they saw.
